Population viability analysis, or PVA, is a simulation-based method used in conservation biology to explore **many possible futures under explicit assumptions** about survival, breeding, habitat limits, and chance events. It asks: if the biology behaves like this, what range of outcomes could happen?

In this practical, each model run is one scenario. The baseline is your reference future, then later runs change one factor so you can see what moved. A good comparison should focus on explaining which input value or assumption changed, and how the population responded.

- **Extinction risk** is the proportion of simulated futures in which population size reaches zero.
- **Abundance** shows where the population tends to finish at the chosen projection horizon.
- **Uncertainty** shows the range of outcomes across simulated futures.
- **Evidence for interpretation** means the outputs support your reasoning and need your judgement.

Use the outputs as evidence for interpretation: compare scenarios, name uncertainty, and be clear about the assumptions behind the model.
