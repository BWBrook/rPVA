The Lord Howe Island woodhen (*Hypotaenidia sylvestris*) is a flightless rail
found nowhere except Lord Howe Island. The island is a small volcanic remnant
about 630 km east of mainland Australia.

## From near-extinction to recovery

Feral pigs damaged native vegetation and preyed on island wildlife after their
introduction in the early nineteenth century. Hunting by people added further
pressure. By the early 1970s, fewer than ten breeding pairs of Woodhens remained,
largely confined to steep upland habitat.

In the early 1980s, managers combined island-wide pig eradication with captive
breeding and release. The population recovered to about 200 birds and remained
near that level from the mid-1990s to the late 2010s. A major rodent-eradication
programme began in 2019. By 2024, surveys reported more than 1,600 Woodhens.

## Your investigation

This recovery did not happen through a single action or a perfectly known
process. In **Phase 1**, you will ask whether a simplified population model can
reproduce the important direction and scale of the historical changes. In
**Phase 2**, you will use that historically grounded model to test plausible
future pressures and management responses.

Agreement with history strengthens confidence in a model, but it does not prove
that every assumption is correct or that one action alone caused the observed
recovery. Keep that distinction in view when you develop conservation advice.
