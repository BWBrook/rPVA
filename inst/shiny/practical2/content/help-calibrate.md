## What Calibrate means here

The supplied Woodhen scenarios represent important stages in the conservation
history. If the model reproduces the direction and approximate scale of the
observed changes, that **supports, but does not prove**, its usefulness for
exploratory future inference. Different assumptions can sometimes produce
similar outcomes, and a modelled comparison cannot by itself prove that one
management action caused the historical recovery.

This is best understood as a **historical reconstruction and plausibility check**,
not independent validation. Some historical observations helped shape
the supplied model settings, so agreement with those same observations cannot
be treated as an independent test.

Use **Scenario details** on each card before interpreting its output. The three
1980 cases can be compared directly because they use the same starting
population, 40-year projection, and number of simulated futures. Read the
abundance, cumulative-risk, extinction-timing, and key-results panels together.

Abundance is shown as total birds. In the two post-eradication scenarios, the
habitat ceiling applies to territory-holding females, used as a proxy for
breeding pairs. Young non-breeding birds can temporarily exceed that ceiling
while waiting for a territory, so total bird abundance is not simply twice the
breeding-territory capacity.

## The short recovery check

The 2019-2024 rodent-eradication scenario begins from the **208 birds released**
after captive management and compares the reconstructed trajectory with the
subsequent survey record. The reported counts are **not fully comparable**:
survey season, coverage, chicks, and the treatment of Mt Gower vary. Use **View
survey record** to inspect those differences rather than reading every point as
an equally precise whole-island census.

Ask whether the simulated recovery has a compatible direction and approximate
scale, including the 2024 known minimum of **1,638 birds**. This short run
**must not be ranked** against the three 40-year cases.

## The common future comparison

The 2025-2100 no-change scenario gives every Phase 2 investigation the same
point of comparison. It is not a prediction that ecological conditions will
remain unchanged. It asks: *if no new pressure or intervention is added, what
does this model project?*

For this future case, also examine the chance of falling **strictly below 100
territory-holding breeding pairs**. Extinction is the final outcome, but a
return to approximately the population scale that preceded rodent eradication
would already demand strong conservation attention. Historical survey totals
and this modelled breeding-pair proxy are different measures, so 100 pairs is
not a direct reconstruction of a past census. It is a **working warning
threshold** adopted for consistent comparison in this practical, not a
universal biological tipping point or a proven minimum viable population.

The model begins near the observed 2024 population scale, but its starting
stage distribution represents approximately 1,650 birds rather than an exact
survey reconstruction. That small difference is less important than being
honest about the distinction between an observed count and a model state.
