## What this comparison asks

This activity asks two linked questions:

1. What happens if a named threat changes the common 2025-2100 future?
2. How much does a compatible management response improve that same threatened
   future?

The app runs three comparable scenarios: the common future, the future with the
threat, and the same threat with management added. This design separates the
effect of the threat from the effect of the response.

## The guided threat families

- **Rat return or loss of control** reduces breeding output through egg or chick
  losses and reduces first-year survival. Established territory-holder
  survival is unchanged. Renewed control recovers a selected fraction of both
  modelled recruitment losses.
- **Loss or degradation of breeding habitat** lowers the number of suitable
  breeding territories. Illustrative routes include climate-driven moisture
  and invertebrate-food changes, and habitat degradation by invasive plants.
  The model represents only the capacity loss; these routes are not predictions.
  Protection or restoration recovers a selected fraction of that capacity loss.
- **Gradual loss of lowland habitat** represents inundation and saline
  intrusion as a decline in breeding-territory capacity from 2025 to 2100. It
  is a habitat pathway, not a spatial sea-level model.
- **More frequent cyclones** combines a higher annual chance of a cyclone with
  lower survival across all model stages and an acute breeding-output loss in
  cyclone years. The breeding-output multiplier represents acute recruitment
  loss; this structure cannot uniquely model juvenile mortality. Emergency
  response does not prevent cyclones. It retains their annual frequency and
  recovers 25%, 50% or 75% of both event-year losses toward the common values.

The selected effect sizes are bounded assumptions for exploration, not
empirical estimates or management promises. The mechanism briefing above the
controls states exactly what changes and what remains fixed.

## Why the comparison is fair

All three scenarios use the same 2025 starting population, 75-year horizon,
1,000 simulated futures and stage structure. Settings not named in the
mechanism briefing remain unchanged.

Read the evidence in this order:

1. compare the common future with the threat;
2. compare the threat with and without management; and
3. compare the managed outcome back with the common future.

## How to read the metrics

Each abundance panel shows the median number of birds and the middle 95% of
1,000 simulated futures. Separate panels prevent overlapping uncertainty bands
from hiding the pattern.

The warning-risk plot shows the proportion of futures that had fallen
**strictly below 100 territory-holding breeding pairs** by each date. The
probability axis runs from 0% to 100%. Complete extinction is still reported in
the comparison table, but it is usually rare under these bounded scenarios.

Fewer than 100 pairs is the shared **working warning threshold** for this
practical. It is approximately the population scale that preceded rodent
eradication, but historical survey totals and this modelled breeding-pair proxy
are different measures. It is not a direct reconstruction of a past census, a
universal minimum viable population, or a proven biological tipping point.

The first-passage plot shows the percentage of all 1,000 futures that first
crossed strictly below 100 pairs in each five-year period. The bars therefore
sum to the overall probability of crossing by 2100. A **conditional median
crossing time** describes only the futures that crossed.

The summary cards report risk differences in **percentage points**. For
example, a change from 26% risk to 1% risk is a reduction of 25 percentage
points.

## What can and cannot be inferred

The 1,000 futures represent process stochasticity under each chosen set of
settings. They do not include uncertainty in parameter values, model form,
observations, threat occurrence, management feasibility, cost or implementation.

A favourable simulated comparison is evidence about the modelled mechanism. It
is not proof that the action will work in the real system, be feasible or
affordable, or be sufficient on its own. Record those limits alongside the
result you would use in conservation advice.
