## Start with a scientific question

Compare is not a ranking table for every run in your project. Begin with one
question, then choose a **matched scenario family** that answers it.

A direct comparison should normally share:

- the same starting population and calendar period;
- the same projection horizon;
- the same number of simulated futures;
- the same no-change assumptions; and
- one clearly stated ecological difference.

For a sensitivity family, only one model assumption changes. For a future
management family, the threatened future changes one or more named ecological
processes and the managed future adds a response to that same threat.

## Read one view at a time

- **Population through time** shows the median and 95% simulation interval.
  The interval describes variation among futures generated under the selected
  settings. It does not include all uncertainty about data, parameters or model
  structure.
- **Warning risk through time** shows the cumulative probability of having
  fallen below 100 territory-holding pairs.
- **Final abundance** gives a compact comparison of the final medians and
  simulation intervals.
- **When the warning threshold was crossed** shows first-passage timing. A
  conditional median crossing year refers only to futures that crossed.
- **Change summary** places final abundance and 100-pair warning risk side by
  side. For sensitivity analysis it summarises the response to one changed
  assumption. For threat and management work it compares a threatened future
  with its managed counterpart.

The 100-pair level is a shared working warning threshold for this practical.
It is close to the population scale before rodent eradication and provides a
consistent comparison point. It is not a universal minimum viable population
or a proven biological tipping point.

## Handle awkward comparisons honestly

Runs from different historical periods or projection horizons can provide
useful context, but they are not automatically one fair experiment. When
Compare displays a context-only warning, do not rank the final values or claim
that their difference estimates a management effect. Return to a matched
family for that purpose.

## Retain evidence selectively

For one claim, a strong minimum evidence set is usually:

1. one plot that exposes the relevant pattern;
2. one compact table that verifies the magnitude;
3. your interpretation; and
4. a caveat that limits the claim appropriately.

Ask whether abundance and warning risk support the same interpretation, what
uncertainty is represented by the simulations, what is omitted, and what
result or assumption would change your advice.

Selecting a plot or table marks it for the comparison note you are building;
selection alone does not save it. **Save comparison notes** will bundle the
selected evidence with your interpretation and caveat, then make that bundle
available in **Evidence Studio**. Evidence Studio is where you will review and
trim the material before exporting your dossier.
