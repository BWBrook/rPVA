## Question

Can the model reproduce the rapid recovery observed after rodent eradication?

The scenario begins with the **208 birds released** after captive management in
late 2019 and projects five years to 2024. That release count is used as the
model starting marker; it is not a whole-island census. Rodent removal is
represented by more available breeding territories, higher early-life
survival, and stronger recruitment. These changes stand in for reduced egg and
chick predation and improved food availability.

The habitat ceiling acts on territory-holding females, used here as a proxy for
breeding pairs. Young non-breeding birds can temporarily occur above that
ceiling while they wait for a territory, so total bird abundance is not simply
twice the territory ceiling.

## How to read it

Compare the model trajectory and uncertainty interval with the plotted survey
record, including the 2024 known minimum of **1,638 birds**. The survey points
differ in timing and coverage, so the broad recovery pattern matters more than
point-for-point agreement. This is a historical reconstruction and
plausibility check, not an independent validation or an estimate of the
separate effect of every ecological process. Do not rank it against the
40-year 1980 scenarios.
