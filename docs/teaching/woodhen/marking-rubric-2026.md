# KPZ308 Assessment Task 1 Marking Rubric, 2026

## Woodhen PVA Conservation Advice Report

## Assessment Criteria

The report is marked out of 25 using these four criteria:

| Criterion | Marks | Assessment Criteria |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| C1 | 5 | Describe the uses and limitations of simulation modelling in conservation decision-making. |
| C2 | 6 | Model stochastic processes and causes of threat. |
| C3 | 8 | Undertake scenario testing to compare the relative effectiveness of alternative management options. |
| C4 | 6 | Translate simulation results and communicate them as conservation recommendations. |

## How The Rubric Is Used

Markers use best-fit judgement. They consider the quality of the relevant
evidence across each criterion, then read the complete report as a connected
scientific argument. A descriptor does not operate as an automatic gate, and
there is no checklist allocation for individual report features. Equivalent
evidence may appear anywhere in a coherent report.

The report is the assessed synthesis. The Evidence Portfolio is the broad
working record, and the evidence dossier is a selected packet of source
material. The report is the routine submission. The Evidence Portfolio and
evidence dossier support your work but are not separately marked.

The expected report length is approximately 2,000 words. Display items and
references are excluded from that word count. MyLO is authoritative for the
published administrative requirements.

## C1: Uses And Limitations Of Simulation Modelling

**Criterion:** Describe the uses and limitations of simulation modelling in conservation decision-making.

**Maximum:** 5 marks.

**What this criterion judges:** How well the report frames the Woodhen
decision, explains the role and limits of population viability analysis (PVA)
evidence, interprets the historical reconstruction, and evaluates limitations
that affect the conclusions.

**Evidence may include:**

- a clear conservation problem, decision, and time frame;
- explanation of how simulations compare risks under alternative futures;
- interpretation of the historical reconstruction as a plausibility check;
- distinction between model output, observations, and independent validation;
- evaluation of data, structural, horizon, or omitted-process limitations; and
- consequences of those limitations for the advice.

**High Distinction:** Develops a precise decision frame and a discerning
account of what the PVA and reconstruction can and cannot support. Limitations
are specific, consequential, and integrated into the interpretation.

**Distinction:** Establishes a clear decision and explains the value of PVA,
the reconstruction, and relevant inference boundaries with only minor gaps.

**Credit:** Gives a relevant decision context and a sound account of model use,
but the reconstruction, limitations, or their consequences receive uneven
analysis.

**Pass:** Shows a basic understanding of the conservation context and PVA, but
model use, reconstruction, or limitations are treated largely at a descriptive
level.

**Fail:** Provides little reliable decision framing or model evaluation, or
treats conditional simulation results as certain forecasts.

## C2: Stochastic Processes And Causes Of Threat

**Criterion:** Model stochastic processes and causes of threat.

**Maximum:** 6 marks.

**What this criterion judges:** How well the report explains the ecological
processes represented in the model, stochasticity, threats, assumptions, and
the methodological choices needed to interpret the investigation.

**Evidence may include:**

- biologically meaningful explanation of population processes and variation;
- the mechanism by which a selected threat affects population outcomes;
- the rationale and prediction for the three-setting one-factor sensitivity;
- identification of settings held constant and settings that differ;
- traceable scenario names, horizons, measures, and comparison logic; and
- acknowledgement of assumptions, data gaps, or processes not represented.

**High Distinction:** Explains relevant ecological and stochastic processes,
threat mechanisms, and modelling choices with precision. Assumptions and
held-constant conditions are traceable and used to support interpretation.

**Distinction:** Gives a clear and biologically sound explanation of major
processes, threat mechanisms, and sensitivity choices, with minor omissions.

**Credit:** Describes the main methods appropriately, but some process,
stochastic, threat, sensitivity, or traceability details remain underdeveloped.

**Pass:** Provides enough method and ecological context to recognise the
investigation, although important processes or comparison settings are unclear.

**Fail:** Methods or ecological mechanisms are absent, inaccurate, or too
unclear to support the conclusions.

## C3: Scenario Testing And Management Effectiveness

**Criterion:** Undertake scenario testing to compare the relative effectiveness of alternative management options.

**Maximum:** 8 marks.

**What this criterion judges:** How well the report designs and interprets
like-for-like comparisons, uses quantitative evidence and uncertainty, and
evaluates management effectiveness and trade-offs.

**Evidence may include:**

- interpretation of the historical reconstruction;
- a three-setting one-factor sensitivity using a no-change baseline plus one
  higher and one lower setting;
- a matched no-change baseline, threatened future, and managed threatened
  future comparison;
- comparable time horizons, settings, measures, and simulation summaries;
- direct comparisons of abundance, risk, timing, and uncertainty where useful;
- interpretation of stochastic variation rather than reliance on point
  estimates alone; and
- management trade-offs, feasibility, or conditions affecting effectiveness.

**High Distinction:** Builds valid, well-matched comparisons and integrates
quantitative results with uncertainty to evaluate management effectiveness.
The analysis identifies important trade-offs and draws careful comparative
inferences.

**Distinction:** Uses appropriate matched comparisons and quantitative
evidence consistently. Management interpretation is strong, with only minor
gaps in uncertainty or trade-off analysis.

**Credit:** Presents broadly valid comparisons, but the justification,
quantification, uncertainty, or management interpretation is uneven.

**Pass:** Provides a recognisable scenario comparison and some relevant
evidence, but weaknesses in matching or interpretation limit the conclusions.

**Fail:** Provides little usable scenario comparison, reports results
inaccurately, or draws management conclusions that the analysis cannot support.

## C4: Conservation Recommendations And Communication

**Criterion:** Translate simulation results and communicate them as conservation recommendations.

**Maximum:** 6 marks.

**What this criterion judges:** How well the report synthesises the
investigation into prioritised, traceable conservation advice and communicates
that advice to scientific peers and relevant stakeholders.

**Evidence may include:**

- a clear and prioritised recommendation or information priority;
- explicit links from advice to matched results and ecological reasoning;
- uncertainty, assumptions, and limitations carried into the recommendation;
- conditions, monitoring evidence, or future results that could change advice;
- selective, legible plots and tables that advance the argument;
- claims supported by relevant evidence and citations; and
- coherent organisation and precise communication for the intended audiences.

**High Distinction:** Synthesises the evidence into prioritised and
well-calibrated advice. The recommendation is traceable to results, uncertainty,
and ecological reasoning, and the complete argument is selective and compelling.

**Distinction:** Gives clear advice that follows logically from the evidence.
Relevant limitations, conditions, displays, and communication are handled well,
with minor gaps.

**Credit:** Gives relevant advice and a mostly coherent argument, but some
evidence links, caveats, conditions, or communication choices remain generic or
uneven.

**Pass:** Offers conservation advice, but it is weakly prioritised, only partly
supported, or obscured by report organisation or display use.

**Fail:** Provides little meaningful advice, disconnects recommendations from
the simulation evidence, or communicates the scientific argument unreliably.

## Reading The Complete Report

The criteria separate important aspects of performance, but strong reports
connect them. Markers consider whether the question, model, historical
reconstruction, three-setting one-factor sensitivity, matched threat-management
comparison, quantitative results, uncertainty, and recommendation form a
coherent whole.

Different report structures, display selections, analytical extensions, and
source choices can achieve the same standard. Quality, relevance, scientific
validity, and clarity matter more than following a template.
