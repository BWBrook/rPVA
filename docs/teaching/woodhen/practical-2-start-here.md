# Practical 2: Start Here

## Before The Class

Practical 2 launches your investigation for the Woodhen PVA Conservation
Advice Report. You will use population viability analysis (PVA) to compare
possible futures for the population. The three-hour class is not intended to
complete the whole report. You will learn the investigation workflow, begin
comparisons, and save evidence that you can examine and develop afterwards.

Before class:

- open the approved app link supplied through MyLO;
- have your Practical 1 notes available, especially your notes on extinction
  probability, abundance, uncertainty, and one-factor comparisons; and
- read the assessment brief and marking rubric supplied through MyLO.

Use only the current app link from MyLO so that your work matches the version
used in class.

## Open Or Resume Your Project

On the **Start** tab you can:

- create a new Woodhen project;
- resume the project saved in this browser;
- import a portable project copy that you downloaded earlier; or
- download a project copy for later use.

Give a new project a name that you will recognise. If you import a copy, check
the project summary before continuing so that you know which investigation you
have opened.

## The Six-Tab Investigation

The app guides you through six connected tabs:

1. **Start** creates, resumes, imports, and summarises your project.
2. **Calibrate** uses the historical reconstruction to examine whether the
   model gives a plausible account of documented recovery and where its
   limitations matter.
3. **Investigate** changes one ecological factor across three settings so you
   can test sensitivity while holding other settings constant.
4. **Compare** examines matched no-change, threatened, and managed threatened
   futures to evaluate management effectiveness.
5. **Evidence Studio** lets you review your Evidence Portfolio, select useful
   plots and tables, add your own notes, and assemble an evidence dossier.
6. **Assessment Task** connects the investigation to the learning outcomes,
   assessment criteria, scientific journey, and report.

Move back and forth as your question develops. A useful comparison keeps the
time horizon, model settings, and output measures like-for-like unless you have
a clear reason to change them.

## Save Work You Want To Keep

The app saves your project in the current browser as you work. Browser storage
is convenient, but it is tied to that browser and device and may be cleared.

Download a portable project copy:

- before leaving the class;
- after completing work that would be difficult to recreate; and
- before changing browser, device, or computer account.

Store the downloaded copy somewhere you can find and back up. When you return,
you can resume the browser copy or import the portable copy. After importing,
check that the project name, notes, and saved evidence are the versions you
expect.

## From Evidence To Report

These three records have different purposes:

- The **Evidence Portfolio** is the broad collection of saved model outputs,
  notes, and details showing where each item came from.
- The **evidence dossier** is a smaller, ordered selection of source material
  assembled in Evidence Studio. It is working material for drafting and
  checking your argument.
- The **report** is your assessed synthesis. You write the interpretation,
  captions, references, and conservation recommendation.

The report is the routine submission. Keep your project copy and dossier as
working records, but do not submit them routinely. The dossier is not
separately marked. Teaching staff may request it only for genuine
clarification, provenance, or academic-integrity review. Follow the
instructions in MyLO if such a review is required.

Use evidence selectively. Your report should explain what the simulation
results show under the model assumptions, how uncertainty affects the
comparison, and why the evidence supports your advice. The in-app **Assessment
Task** tab provides guidance while you work.

## Continue After The Practical

After class, use the approved MyLO app link and resume or import your project.
You may refine your question, repeat a comparison, develop an intellectual
extension, consult wider Woodhen research, or improve a display when this
strengthens the scientific argument.

Keep direct comparisons like-for-like. Record why you changed a setting, what
you expected beforehand, and what the result means for your recommendation.
Treat simulated outcomes as conditional on model structure, data, settings, and
stochastic variation.

## If Something Goes Wrong

- If the app reports that there is no project, return to **Start** and create,
  resume, or import one.
- If work from another session is missing, import your most recent portable
  project copy.
- If a result looks unexpected, check the selected scenario, factor setting,
  time horizon, and held-constant settings before interpreting it.
- If the app stops responding, preserve any downloadable project copy, reload
  the approved MyLO link, and resume or import the project.
- If you cannot recover your work, record the problem and contact the teaching
  team through the support route listed in MyLO. Include the tab you were using
  and the action that preceded the problem, but do not include passwords or
  other private information.
