# KPZ308 Assessment Task 1, 2026

## Woodhen PVA Conservation Advice Report

## The Task

Act as a present-day conservation adviser for the Lord Howe Island Woodhen.
Write an evidence-led report that recommends a conservation response or
information priority. Use population viability analysis (PVA) to compare
possible futures. Base your advice on the supplied case material, your Practical
2 investigation, ecological reasoning, and any relevant wider research you
choose to use.

Write approximately 2,000 words. Display items and references are excluded from
the word count.

Make the conservation problem and decision clear. Use model results as
conditional evidence rather than certain forecasts: explain important
assumptions and uncertainty, and identify conditions or new evidence that could
change your recommendation.

## Intended Learning Outcomes

This task assesses your ability to:

1. Discuss the requirements for success in effective species conservation by integrating theory and case studies.
2. Diagnose and solve biodiversity problems by applying fundamental principles from population and community ecology, genetics and ecosystem science.
3. Evaluate the causes of uncertainty and controversy in the development of strategies for species conservation.
4. Communicate a conservation problem to scientific peers and relevant stakeholders.

## Complete The Scientific Journey

Your investigation must bring together the following three forms of evidence.
Use the same time horizon and matched model settings when making direct
comparisons, unless the purpose of a comparison requires otherwise and you
explain the difference.

### Historical Reconstruction

Use the supplied historical reconstruction to examine how well the model
represents the documented management history. Explain what agreement or
disagreement with observed recovery can support, which assumptions matter, and
why the reconstruction is a plausibility check rather than independent
validation. Independent validation would require evidence outside the
model-building workflow.

### Three-Setting One-Factor Sensitivity

Examine one named ecological factor using a three-setting one-factor
sensitivity:

- the no-change baseline;
- one higher setting for the factor; and
- one lower setting for the same factor.

Keep other model settings and the time horizon matched. Before examining the
results, state the biological reason for selecting the factor and the direction
of change you expect. Then compare what changed, including uncertainty, and
explain how the result affects confidence in your advice.

### Threat-Management Comparison

Use at least one matched comparison of:

- the no-change baseline;
- a plausible threatened future; and
- the corresponding managed threatened future.

Compare like-for-like outputs at the same horizon and under matched settings.
Explain the threat mechanism, the management response, the size and uncertainty
of the response, and any ecological or implementation conditions that matter.

## Develop Your Investigation

Use the practical as a starting point for a focused investigation. Questions
that may help include:

- Which present-day conservation decision or question will your report address?
- Which historical comparison most affects your confidence in the model?
- Which ecological factor will your sensitivity analysis examine, and why?
- Which plausible threat and corresponding management response will you compare?
- Which abundance, risk, timing, and uncertainty measures answer your question?
- Which model assumption, data gap, or omitted process most affects your advice?
- What future evidence or outcome would change your recommendation?

Your intellectual extension is flexible. You may develop the analysis,
ecological interpretation, management framing, or supporting research in the
direction that best strengthens your argument. There is no quota for additional
scenarios or wider sources. Wider Woodhen research is encouraged where it helps
you evaluate ecological context, assumptions, threat mechanisms, management
feasibility, or uncertainty. Select credible sources because they support a
claim, not to meet a source count.

## Portfolio, Dossier, And Report

The **Evidence Portfolio** is your broad saved working record in the project.
It can contain model-generated plots and tables, your notes, and details
showing where saved evidence came from.

An **evidence dossier** is a smaller, ordered packet of source material that you
select in Evidence Studio. The evidence dossier is working material for
developing and checking your argument. Keep it for your own records.

The **student-authored report** is the assessed synthesis. The report is the
routine submission; the dossier is not routinely submitted. You must write the
interpretations, captions, references, and conservation advice in the report.
The dossier is not separately marked. Teaching staff may request it only for
genuine clarification, provenance, or academic-integrity review. Follow the
instructions in MyLO if such a review is required.

## Build A Coherent Argument

Several organisations can work:

- A conventional scientific sequence can move from context and question
  through methods, results, interpretation, and recommendation.
- A decision-led sequence can begin with the conservation decision, compare
  options, examine uncertainty, and finish with conditional advice.
- An evidence-led sequence can organise the argument around the historical,
  sensitivity, and threat-management evidence before integrating the
  recommendation.
- Another coherent organisation may suit your particular question better.

Choose headings and allocate space to serve your argument. No particular
headings or section word allocations are prescribed. Make the links among the
question, model, comparisons, evidence, uncertainty, and advice easy to follow.

## Select Evidence Carefully

Use quantitative evidence where it informs the decision. Relevant measures may
include extinction probability, abundance, uncertainty intervals, extinction
timing, trajectory behaviour, and model diagnostics. Make clear which values
are simulation results and what they mean under the model assumptions.

A selection of four to five principal display items is a soft planning guide,
not a marking threshold. Select the plots and tables that best advance your argument. A
well-designed multi-panel figure may communicate several connected results.
Make every display legible, refer to it in the text, explain what comparison it
shows, and interpret its relevance. You may use an app display directly or
create a clearer synthesis from exported data.

## Final Scientific Review

Before finalising your report, ask:

- Is the conservation problem, decision, and role of PVA clear?
- Have I interpreted the historical reconstruction without treating it as
  independent validation?
- Does my sensitivity evidence change one named factor across three settings
  while holding other settings constant?
- Does my threat-management evidence compare matched no-change, threatened,
  and managed threatened futures?
- Are quantitative comparisons like-for-like, and is uncertainty explicit?
- Does each display advance the argument and receive interpretation in the text?
- Does the advice follow from the results, ecological reasoning, assumptions,
  and uncertainty?
- Have I stated the conditions or evidence that could change my recommendation?
- Do references support the claims to which they are attached?

This review supports scientific accuracy and communication. It is not a
separate marking checklist.
