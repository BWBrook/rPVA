# Practical 1 Instructor Beat Sheet

## Teaching stance

Lead with the app and keep the worksheet light. Students should test one
assumption at a time, compare outputs, and explain what the evidence supports.
Circulate during the self-paced period rather than moving the room through
fixed checkpoints.

The three-hour class uses approximately one hour for PVA teaching and a guided
GUI introduction, followed by approximately two hours of self-paced Practical
1 work while the instructor and demonstrators circulate.

## Your route through Practical 1

Start with the five **Core** model missions: Baseline compass, Island ceiling,
Individual chance, Environmental variability, and Rescue attempt. Then go to **Compare**
and build a three-run evidence set containing baseline, one risk mechanism, and
one intervention or vortex run.

When the Core route is complete, **Explore Further** remains available for
genetic pressure, catastrophes, the extinction vortex, advanced controls,
alternative comparisons, and optional plot downloads.

This browser session is temporary. Before closing the tab, request both your
comparison CSV and your evidence-notes Markdown file. Refreshing, closing the
tab, or allowing the session to expire clears the in-app run history,
selection, progress, and interpretation.

## Beats

### First hour: teaching and GUI introduction

- Frame PVA as comparison of possible futures under explicit assumptions.
- Run `Start baseline` and confirm that the student-created run is baseline 1.
- Locate extinction risk, abundance, uncertainty, mission help, and plot help.

### Next two hours: guided self-paced app work

- Prompt students through the five Core missions in order.
- Ask after each run: what changed, which output moved, and what caveat remains?
- At Compare, verify exactly baseline plus one risk plus one intervention or
  vortex and an interpretation before both downloads.
- Offer Explore Further only after the Core destination is clear.

## Roaming prompts

- Which assumption did you change, exactly?
- Which output moved first: risk, abundance, uncertainty, or a mixture?
- What evidence in the app supports that statement?
- What would be misleading if you reported only one output?
- What caveat would stop you from overselling this result?

## Common misconceptions

| Misconception | Coaching response |
| --- | --- |
| `PE = 0` means no risk | It means no extinction was observed in this simulation horizon. Ask about uncertainty and assumptions. |
| The highest median is automatically best | Check extinction risk and uncertainty before calling a result preferable. |
| More mechanisms means a better model | Each assumption should be interpretable; more is not automatically better. |
| The export is the answer | The export records evidence. The scientific work is the explanation. |
| Duplicate reruns improve evidence | A targeted comparison is stronger than filling the table with repeated clicks. |

## Time triage

If setup takes longer than expected, protect the five Core mission route and
Compare. Explore Further can be left optional. If downloads fail, ask students
to retry once, then record the visible comparison as fallback evidence.

## Endpoint check

Before students leave, confirm that they have the comparison CSV and evidence notes
Markdown file, or labelled fallback equivalents, plus a short
interpretation and caveat. Remind them that refreshing, closing the tab, or
session expiry clears in-app history, selection, progress, and interpretation.
