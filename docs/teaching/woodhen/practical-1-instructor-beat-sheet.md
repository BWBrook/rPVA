# Practical 1 Instructor Beat Sheet

## Teaching Stance

Lead with the app. Keep the worksheet light. The goal is for students to test
assumptions, compare outputs, and explain ecological mechanisms in their own
words.

Use fallback material only if the app path is unavailable, slow, or losing
evidence downloads. The fallback should protect the same learning objective,
not replace the app-led design when the app is working.

## Barry Priming Points

- "A model is a way to ask what follows from assumptions."
- "Today you are not trying to find the answer. You are learning how outputs
  respond when assumptions change."
- "For every run, ask: what changed, what output moved, and what caveat
  remains?"
- "The Woodhen practical will use the same habits with a real conservation
  case."

## Recap Beats

After Mission 3:

- Ask students to name the first output that changed when carrying capacity was
  added.
- Ask whether demographic stochasticity changed the median, the spread, or both.
- Reinforce that one-factor comparisons are easier to interpret.

After Mission 6:

- Ask students to separate median abundance from extinction risk.
- Ask whether a rare event can matter even when ordinary years look acceptable.
- Reinforce that zero observed extinction is a simulation outcome, not proof of
  safety.

Final wrap:

- Ask for one example where abundance and risk did not tell the same story.
- Ask which caveat they would carry into a management recommendation.
- Preview Practical 2 as the same reasoning pattern applied to Woodhen
  scenarios.

## Roaming Prompts

- "Which assumption did you change, exactly?"
- "Which output moved first: extinction risk, median abundance, or uncertainty?"
- "What evidence in the app supports that statement?"
- "What would be misleading if you reported only the median?"
- "Is this a risk story, an abundance story, an uncertainty story, or a mixed
  story?"
- "What caveat would stop you from overselling this result?"

## Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | Coaching response |
| --- | --- |
| `PE = 0` means no risk | "It means no extinctions were observed in this simulation horizon. Ask about uncertainty and unmodelled processes." |
| The highest median is automatically best | "Check extinction risk and uncertainty before calling a scenario best." |
| More mechanisms means a better model | "A model is useful when each assumption is justified and interpretable." |
| The export is the answer | "The export records evidence. The scientific work is your explanation." |
| Duplicate reruns improve evidence | "A targeted comparison is stronger than filling the table with repeated clicks." |
| The invented rail is the Woodhen | "The rail is a teaching model. The Woodhen case comes next." |

## Time Triage

If setup takes longer than expected:

- keep Missions 1, 2, 4, 6, and 7;
- turn Mission 3 into a short instructor recap;
- demonstrate Mission 5 if there is not enough time for every group to run it;
- preserve Compare and Transfer.

If downloads fail:

- ask students to retry once;
- then have them copy the visible comparison table into the worksheet;
- switch to the fallback pack only if the download issue is widespread or
  costing class time.

If students race ahead:

- ask them to rerun one mission with exactly one slider changed;
- ask them to explain why that comparison is stronger than changing two
  controls;
- ask them to choose the clearest three-run comparison for a short written
  explanation.

## Endpoint Check

Before leaving Practical 1, each student or group should have:

- at least three compared runs, including the baseline;
- one written example of an assumption change and output response;
- one caveat about uncertainty or model limits;
- the comparison CSV and evidence-notes file, if downloads worked;
- answers to the three Transfer questions;
- a clear link from Practical 1 habits to Practical 2 Woodhen scenario
  comparison.
